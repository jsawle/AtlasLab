# T13 Owen (instructional technology coach): review of School Atlas 5.0.0-beta.43

Judged only from the packet (notes.txt, facts.txt, screenshots 01 to 27) and the published threat model (SECURITY.md). I have not used the live app.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. No privacy statement and no accessibility statement to hand to the technology office
- **Place:** nowhere in the app. 01-first-open.png shows the whole first screen: "School Atlas", "Sign in", the toolbar, the version button and "Powered by Esri". No link to either statement. facts.txt confirms: "There is no privacy statement, no accessibility statement".
- **Why it matters to me:** these are the first two documents the technology office asks for. Without them I cannot put the tool forward, however good the answers are. And the answers look good: no account, no cookies, no server of its own, nothing stored before a save.
- **How serious:** stops the recommendation (not the lesson).
- **Suggested change:** a one-page plain privacy statement (what is stored on the device, what goes to Esri's services, what happens on sign-in, what happens to a student's spreadsheet) and a one-page accessibility statement (what has been tested, known gaps, who to contact), both linked from the first screen.

### 2. No teacher how-to and no help link in the app
- **Place:** 01-first-open.png, top bar and toolbar. There is no Help or "?" anywhere. facts.txt: "no teacher how-to guide and no help link inside the app".
- **Why it matters:** I recommend a tool only if I can teach it in a short session and leave teachers something to follow. Here I would have to write the guide myself, for a test version that is changing (it went from beta.38 to beta.43 between the threat model and this packet).
- **How serious:** slows it down a lot. For a hundred teachers it stops it.
- **Suggested change:** a two-page "first lesson" guide (open a map, turn layers on and off, add a layer, save, reopen, hand in) and a Help link in the top bar that opens it.

### 3. Nowhere to go when it breaks
- **Place:** 02-version-about.png, Versions panel. The only name in the app is "Created by Jason Sawle." SECURITY.md says "Tell Jason Sawle directly rather than opening a public issue" but gives no address or form.
- **Why it matters:** teachers message me when something breaks. I need to know where I pass it on, and whether anyone answers in term time. One named person with no contact route is not something I can tell the technology office is supported.
- **How serious:** stops the recommendation.
- **Suggested change:** a "Report a problem" link in the app (an email address or form), and a line saying what response to expect while it is a test version.

### 4. It is a test version at a personal web address, with test content, and the address is likely to change
- **Place:** 01-first-open.png, bottom right: "v5.0.0-beta.43". facts.txt: "a test version, not yet an official product"; address jsawle.github.io/AtlasLab/; the maps "come from a test content group". SECURITY.md says its own domain is wanted and "Hosting is being handled separately."
- **Why it matters:** any link I put in a guide, and any link a teacher posts for a class, breaks when the address moves. The technology office will also ask why a classroom tool sits on an individual's page. Test content can change or vanish before a planned lesson.
- **How serious:** stops a district-wide recommendation. Fine for a small trial with willing teachers.
- **Suggested change:** say on the first screen or in the Versions panel that this is a trial version, what may change, and where the stable address will be. Do not ask districts to roll it out until the address is settled.

### 5. It is not clear who provides the tool
- **Place:** 01-first-open.png: "School Atlas" and "Powered by Esri". 02-version-about.png: "White-label build of the MapMaker app: every MapMaker tool, local save, CSV data, ArcGIS Online sign-in. Created by Jason Sawle." 04-tool-open.png: a category called "National Geographic Explorers".
- **Why it matters:** the technology office's first question is "who is the vendor, and who do we hold to the privacy terms?" From the screen I see four names (School Atlas, MapMaker, Esri, National Geographic) and one person. I cannot answer the question. "White-label build" is also not a phrase a teacher will understand.
- **How serious:** slows it down. Stops approval in a district that needs a signed data agreement.
- **Suggested change:** one plain sentence in the Versions panel and in the privacy statement: who runs this, who is responsible for it, and how it relates to MapMaker and Esri.

### 6. The Sign in button is the most prominent thing on screen, and nothing says which accounts are meant or who sets them up
- **Place:** 01-first-open.png, top right "Sign in". 24-tool-save.png, the "Sign in" tile. 26-sign-in.png: "Sign in with ArcGIS" / "Use your ArcGIS account to sign in. Signing in lets you save maps to your ArcGIS Online account." / "You don't need an account to use the app or to save maps on this device."
- **Why it matters:** the last sentence is exactly right and I would quote it to teachers. But students click "Sign in" on any page. The packet does not say whether a school's ArcGIS accounts work here, whether a student could make a personal account, or whether the district can switch the button off. SECURITY.md says "Sign-in is off unless a config sets a client ID", so it seems it can be off, but nothing tells a district how to ask for that. SECURITY.md also lists as still open "No real signed-in test" (the sign-in changes were checked with a made-up account, not a real one).
- **How serious:** slows it down. In a district with no ArcGIS accounts, a class of students stuck on a sign-in page costs lesson time.
- **Suggested change:** offer a no-sign-in version for districts that do not use ArcGIS accounts and say so in the setup notes. In the dialog, add who the sign-in is for ("for schools that already have ArcGIS Online accounts"). Complete the real signed-in test before any district uses sign-in. What Continue leads to needs checking in the live app.

### 7. No class roster and no Google Classroom or learning platform link, and no stated hand-in route
- **Place:** facts.txt: "It cannot be opened with a class roster and has no link to Google Classroom or another learning platform". 04-tool-open.png shows "Share" on each map card. 24-tool-save.png shows saving to an ".amap" file.
- **Why it matters:** teachers will ask me "how do I set this and how do they hand it in?" The parts are there: post a map's Share link in Classroom, students save a file and attach it. But nobody has written that down, and the packet does not show what Share produces or whether a teacher can open thirty handed-in .amap files in any quick way.
- **How serious:** slows it down.
- **Suggested change:** put the Classroom routine in the how-to (post the link, save the file, attach it). Whether a student's own changed map can be shared as a link, or only as a file, needs checking in the live app.

### 8. Saving on shared Chromebooks is not explained, and work may appear lost
- **Place:** 24-tool-save.png: "Save your map as an .amap file, wherever you choose. No sign-in needed. After the first save, Save updates the same file." 02-version-about.png: "Maps saved on this device, newest first, kept in this browser." 04-tool-open.png, "My map files" tab.
- **Why it matters:** in my schools students use cart Chromebooks and get a different one each lesson. "My map files" is kept "on this device", so the list may be empty next lesson unless the file went to Google Drive. That will be my most common message: "my map is gone". Whether the list follows a student's Chromebook sign-in to another device needs checking on a managed Chromebook; the packet does not say.
- **How serious:** slows the lesson, and on a cart it can lose a student's work.
- **Suggested change:** in the Save panel and the how-to, tell students to save into Google Drive, and say plainly that "My map files" is a shortcut on this device, not a store of the work.

### 9. The save message in the screenshot tells a Chrome user to use Chrome
- **Place:** 25-save-pressed.png, "Save your map" window: "This browser saves files to its Downloads folder. To choose a folder, use Chrome or Edge."
- **Why it matters:** notes.txt says the screenshots were made in Chrome and that the test browser had no "choose where" dialog, so this may be an effect of the test set-up. If a managed Chromebook behaves the same way, a teacher on Chrome is told to use Chrome, and files land in Downloads, which on many school Chromebooks is cleared at sign-out.
- **How serious:** minor if it is only the test set-up; slows the lesson if it happens on real Chromebooks. Needs checking in the live app on a district-managed Chromebook.
- **Suggested change:** check on a managed Chromebook. If the message can appear in Chrome, change it so it does not name the browser the person is already using.

### 10. The threat model is honest but is not what the technology office needs, and it is out of date
- **Place:** SECURITY.md. "Written for 5.0.0-beta.38 (13 November 2026)"; the app in the packet is beta.43. It calls saved files ".mmap"; the app says ".amap" (24-tool-save.png). It lists eight open items, among them: other pages at the same web address can read the app's list of recent maps; the sign-in token still appears in the address of some requests when signed in; and "One account, merges without a second reviewer".
- **Why it matters:** cybersecurity is the top concern of the people I report to. I respect a document that lists what is still open, and I would hand it over. But it is written for developers, it does not match the version on screen, and the open items will read as reasons to wait unless there is a plain summary and a date for each.
- **How serious:** slows approval.
- **Suggested change:** bring it up to the current version and file name, and add a half-page summary in plain words: what is collected (nothing by the app), what is risky only if you sign in, and what is being fixed and when.

### 11. No published list of web addresses to allow, and no stated device requirements
- **Place:** facts.txt lists the Esri addresses and adds "map tile servers named by a map". The walk in notes.txt also contacted "fly.maptiles.arcgis.com" and "rhvpkkiftonktxq3.svcs9.arcgis.com", which are not in that list by name. 22-to-3d.png: 3D took 45 seconds on the test machine (no graphics card).
- **Why it matters:** if the district's filter blocks one of these, the map is blank and the teacher thinks the tool is broken. The filter team needs one list they can paste in. "Servers named by a map" is open-ended. I also need to tell teachers whether 3D is usable on our older Chromebooks; the packet cannot tell me, since the test machine was not a Chromebook.
- **How serious:** stops the lesson where the filter blocks it; otherwise minor.
- **Suggested change:** publish a short "for your technology office" note: addresses to allow (github.io page, *.arcgis.com, *.arcgisonline.com, plus any others the supplied maps use), supported browsers and the oldest Chromebook tested, and how 3D performed on it. Needs checking on real Chromebooks.

### 12. The version is easy to find, but the list of what changed is not
- **Place:** 01-first-open.png, bottom right "v5.0.0-beta.43"; 02-version-about.png, the Versions panel: "Version numbers for this app and each of its parts."
- **Why it matters:** the version button is a real help: a teacher can tell me the number in a message. But the panel says what each part is, not what changed or when, and has no date. facts.txt says release notes are in the code repository's README; the screenshot shows no link to them from the panel (the panel scrolls, so the bottom of it needs checking in the live app). Five test versions in about a day means my guide can go stale without my knowing.
- **How serious:** minor.
- **Suggested change:** add the release date and a "What's new" link to the Versions panel, with two or three plain lines per version.

One more item I could not judge: in 27-open-another-unsaved.png another map is chosen while there are unsaved changes, and the screenshot shows no warning. It may have been taken before a warning appeared. Whether the app warns before throwing away unsaved work needs checking in the live app; if it does not, that will be a regular "I lost my map" message.

## What works well for me

- No account, no sign-in, nothing to install: it opens from a link, so nobody has to set anything up before first use.
- The sign-in window says it straight: "You don't need an account to use the app or to save maps on this device."
- It collects very little: no cookies, no server of its own, and nothing stored in the browser until a map is saved or opened from a file (facts.txt and the storage check at the end of notes.txt).
- The version number is always on screen (bottom right) and opens a table of every part, and a threat model is published that lists its own open problems.

## Would I recommend it?

Not to a hundred teachers as it stands, though I would happily run a small trial with a few; the single change that would matter most is a short "for schools" page linked from the first screen that holds the privacy statement, the accessibility statement, a two-page teacher how-to and a contact for problems.
