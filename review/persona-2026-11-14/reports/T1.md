# T1 review: Mark, veteran 8th-grade US History teacher

Reviewed as Mark: 14 years in, 50-minute periods, five sections, student Chromebooks, Google Classroom, has used Google Maps but never ArcGIS or MapMaker. Judged only from the packet (notes, 27 screenshots, facts). I applied my four checks to the app itself: can I see in two minutes what students would do, can I run it tomorrow on ten minutes' preparation, does a task fit one period, and is anything on the screen in my way.

One limit on the whole review: the only map the packet shows opened is an earth science one ("Exploring earthquakes and their causes (Guided Lesson)"). I never see one of the United States History maps open, so I cannot say what my students would actually look at. That needs checking in the live app before I could decide.

## Findings (most serious first)

### 1. A "Guided Lesson" opens as a bare map, with nothing telling students what to do
- **Place:** 06-map-opened.png, whole screen; the title bar reads "Exploring earthquakes and their causes (Guided Lesson)" with a small black "i" button beside it.
- **What is there:** The map, dots and lines, and the toolbar. No question, no steps, no instructions. The card in Open map promised "What causes earthquakes, and how do they affect people?" but that question is not on the screen once the map is open.
- **Why it matters to me:** My first check is whether I can see in two minutes what students will do. Here I cannot. If a thing is called a guided lesson I expect the guiding to be on the screen. Without it I have to write the task myself, which is more than ten minutes.
- **Seriousness:** Stops the lesson (for a teacher walking in cold).
- **Cannot tell from the packet:** what the "i" button shows. It was not clicked in the walk. If the steps are behind it, that needs checking in the live app, and the button needs a word on it.
- **Suggested change:** When a lesson map opens, show its question and steps in a panel straight away, and label the button (for example "About this map") rather than a bare "i".

### 2. No help, no teacher guide, and nothing on the first screen that says where to start
- **Place:** 01-first-open.png, whole screen; facts.txt ("no teacher how-to guide and no help link inside the app").
- **What is there:** A world map centred on the Atlantic and eleven buttons. No welcome line, no "start here", no help button.
- **Why it matters to me:** I have never used this kind of tool. Nothing here tells me why it beats the PBS or Smithsonian material I already use, or what a first lesson looks like. I would have to click around on my own time, and I coach after school.
- **Seriousness:** Slows it down a lot; for many teachers like me it ends the visit.
- **Suggested change:** A one-line prompt on first open pointing at "Open map", and a Help link to a one-page teacher guide ("a first lesson in one period").

### 3. Handing work in: the saved file cannot be looked at in Google Classroom
- **Place:** 24-tool-save.png and 25-save-pressed.png, Save panel and "Save your map" window; facts.txt ("no link to Google Classroom or another learning platform").
- **What is there:** Save makes a file ending ".amap". The other choices are "Save as…", "Screenshot", "Print" and "Sign in".
- **Why it matters to me:** With five sections I collect well over a hundred pieces of work. An .amap file attached in Classroom is something I would have to download and open in the app one at a time. The Screenshot is the only thing here I can grade quickly, and nothing tells students that is the one to turn in.
- **Seriousness:** Slows it down (every assignment, every section).
- **Cannot tell from the packet:** whether a student can share their own finished map as a link. "Share" appears only on the cards of the built-in maps.
- **Suggested change:** Say on the Save panel which choice is for handing in (for example under Screenshot: "A picture you can turn in"), and if a student's map can be shared as a link, put that choice here too.

### 4. The Save panel's explanations do not appear in the screenshot
- **Place:** 24-tool-save.png, Save panel, compared with the words listed for that step in notes.txt.
- **What is there:** The notes list helpful words: "Save your map as an .amap file, wherever you choose. No sign-in needed. After the first save, Save updates the same file." In the picture I see only the tile names: "Save map", "Save as…", "Screenshot", "Print", "Sign in".
- **Why it matters to me:** "No sign-in needed" is exactly what I want students to read before they hit "Sign in" and get stuck. If it only shows when the pointer rests on a tile, most of them will never see it.
- **Seriousness:** Slows it down. Needs checking in the live app whether these words are visible without hovering.
- **Suggested change:** Print the one-line explanation under each tile name.

### 5. It is a test version with test content
- **Place:** every screenshot, bottom right "v5.0.0-beta.43"; facts.txt ("a test version, not yet an official product"; maps "come from a test content group").
- **What is there:** A beta label on screen at all times, hosted at a github.io address.
- **Why it matters to me:** If I build a lesson around a map and post it to five sections, I need that map to be there next week and next year. Nothing tells me whether it will be. I also cannot tell whether my district's filter allows this address on student Chromebooks; that needs checking at my school, not in the app.
- **Seriousness:** Slows it down; would stop me using it for anything graded.
- **Suggested change:** A plain sentence in the app saying what "beta" means for a teacher: whether maps and links will keep working.

### 6. The Legend panel is empty
- **Place:** 12-tool-legend.png, top right panel "Legend".
- **What is there:** The heading "Legend" and a blank box, while the map has yellow, red, blue and grey dots and white lines with three layers switched on.
- **Why it matters to me:** Reading a legend is the first map skill I ask for. If students cannot find out what the red dots mean, the task stops at the first question.
- **Seriousness:** Stops the lesson if it is real. Needs checking in the live app: the picture may have been taken before the legend filled in.
- **Suggested change:** If it is slow, show "Loading…"; if a layer has no legend, say so in words rather than leaving the box blank.

### 7. Getting 30 students onto the same map: the Share button is not shown working
- **Place:** 04-tool-open.png, "Share" beside "Open map" on each card.
- **What is there:** facts.txt says a map can be shared as a link. The walk never clicks Share, so I do not see the link or what a student gets when they open it.
- **Why it matters to me:** This is my ten-minute preparation. If I can paste one link into Google Classroom and every student lands on the "1775-1776 Revolutionary Battles" map, I can run it tomorrow. If they land on the world map and have to find it among "121 maps" across 13 pages, I lose five minutes a period.
- **Seriousness:** Slows it down. Needs checking in the live app.
- **Suggested change:** Make sure the link opens straight onto the map, and say so on the button's result ("Link copied. Students who open it go straight to this map").

### 8. The row of small picture-only buttons under each layer
- **Place:** 08-tool-layers.png and 09-layer-menu.png, "Map layers" panel, under "Historic Major Earthquakes".
- **What is there:** Eight small symbols with no words, one of them a trash can. A second trash can sits at the top of the panel beside the close button.
- **Why it matters to me:** I cannot tell a student "click the third little picture". And a trash can within easy reach of an 8th grader means somebody deletes a layer, or all of them, in the first five minutes, and I do not see an undo.
- **Seriousness:** Slows it down.
- **Cannot tell from the packet:** whether names appear when the pointer rests on a symbol, and whether delete asks "are you sure". Needs checking in the live app.
- **Suggested change:** Put words on the two or three students need (Table, Filter, About), tuck the rest and the trash cans behind "More".

### 9. The toolbar disappears when the table or the elevation chart opens
- **Place:** 11-layer-table.png and 17-tool-profile.png, bottom of the map; a small unlabelled square of nine dots appears at the left instead.
- **What is there:** The eleven labelled buttons are gone while the table or "Elevation profile" fills the bottom of the screen.
- **Why it matters to me:** "Where did my buttons go?" times thirty. I would be walking the room fixing this instead of teaching.
- **Seriousness:** Slows it down.
- **Cannot tell from the packet:** that the nine-dot square brings the toolbar back. I assume so; needs checking.
- **Suggested change:** Keep the toolbar in view above the table or chart, or label the nine-dot button "Tools".

### 10. Opening another map with unsaved work: no warning visible
- **Place:** 27-open-another-unsaved.png, Open map window with the second card's "Open map" pressed.
- **What is there:** The step is described as "With unsaved changes, another map chosen". The picture shows no "you have unsaved changes" message.
- **Why it matters to me:** A student who loses 30 minutes of work at minute 40 has nothing to turn in, and I have a parent email.
- **Seriousness:** Stops the lesson for that student if no warning exists. Needs checking in the live app: the picture may have been taken before a warning appeared.
- **Suggested change:** A plain warning with "Save first", "Open without saving" and "Cancel".

### 11. Adding a spreadsheet: the points are not visible straight after "Add to map"
- **Place:** 15-csv-added.png, whole map; compare 17-tool-profile.png and 23-back-2d.png where four coloured circles are there.
- **What is there:** After pressing "Add to map" the picture shows no new points and no message, though the Map layers count went from 5 to 6. The circles show up in later pictures.
- **Why it matters to me:** A student who sees nothing happen will add it again, and again.
- **Seriousness:** Minor to slows it down. Needs checking in the live app: this may only be the test machine drawing slowly.
- **Suggested change:** A short confirmation ("Added 4 points") when the layer goes on.

### 12. Two tools I would want are not proven in the packet, and some wording is not for my room
- **Place:** notes.txt after 16-tool-measure.png ("measure-distance FAILED"); 22-to-3d.png (shown "45 seconds later"); 14-csv-dropped.png; 07-map-popup.png.
- **What is there:** The Distance button could not be pressed in the recorded walk, so I never see a measured distance. 3D took 45 seconds on the test machine, which had no graphics card. Wording: "Colour points by" and "Colour & size" (British spelling), and a pop-up line "USGS PAGER ALERT: No PAGER Issued".
- **Why it matters to me:** Measuring distance (how far did an army march) is the one tool I would reach for first in history, so I need to know it works. I would keep students out of 3D until I had tried it on a school Chromebook. The spelling gets "that's spelled wrong" from 8th graders; the PAGER line means nothing to them or me.
- **Seriousness:** Distance and 3D: needs checking in the live app on a Chromebook; cannot judge. Wording: minor.
- **Suggested change:** Confirm Distance on a Chromebook; use US spelling when the app is in miles; the pop-up wording belongs to the map's author rather than the app.

## What works well for me
- No account, no install, no sign-in: it opens at a web address and the sign-in window says "You don't need an account to use the app or to save maps on this device."
- The toolbar buttons have plain words under them ("Open map", "Map layers", "Measure", "Save"), and it starts in miles and feet for a US classroom.
- Open map has a "United States History" category (22 maps) and cards with a sentence I can read in seconds, such as "Explore how the geography of North America shaped the first phase (1775-1776) of the American Revolution and why location mattered in the fight for independence."
- The elevation chart reads in plain English ("The line is 120 mi long. Its highest point is 10,607 ft and its lowest 3,950 ft.") and would work for showing why the Appalachians or the Rockies mattered.

## Would I use it?
Not tomorrow as it stands, though I would come back to it: the single change that matters most is that a map, when it opens, tells students on the screen what to do with it (finding 1), because that is what lets me post one link in Google Classroom and teach from it in one period.
